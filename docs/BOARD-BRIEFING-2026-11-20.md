# AI in the Wild · NKU College of Informatics Board Conversation
**Friday, November 20, 2026** · Discussion guide (not an endorsement or institutional research finding)

## Objective
Help board members distinguish **AI activity, operational value, evidence quality, and workforce implications**. Demonstrate a continuously expanding case library with a separate, unverified discovery pipeline.

## Suggested 15-minute live flow
**0:00–2:00 — Frame the question.** “The real question is not whether organizations are using AI. It's which workflows improve, what evidence supports that, and what talent organizations need.”

**2:00–5:00 — A familiar operational use case.** Search for **Walmart**, open Professional View, and show reported shift-planning improvements. Ask: “If this happened at your organization, what would you actually measure before scaling?” The 90-to-30-minute figure is company-reported, not independently audited.

**5:00–8:00 — Independent productivity evidence.** Search for **Microsoft, Accenture**, then **Multi-company customer support study**, and switch to Compare Table. Explain that controlled/observational research may be more useful than vendor ROI claims. Discuss 26.08% task-completion gains (three developer field experiments) versus average 15% more issues resolved hourly in customer support (QJE study). These are different outcomes and cannot be combined.

**8:00–11:00 — What goes wrong.** Filter Outcome = Negative; inspect **McDonald's** stopped drive-through pilot and **iTutorGroup** EEOC settlement, and contrast with **Air Canada**. Discuss evaluation, accessibility, bias checks, escalation and accountability. iTutorGroup was automated screening; the sources do not establish generative AI.

**11:00–13:00 — Workforce implications.** Switch to **Student View**. Learning questions now appear directly on each card. Ask board members to identify one role-specific skill they want new graduates to demonstrate: workflow mapping, evaluation design, data literacy, governance, or human escalation.

**13:00–15:00 — Invite collaboration.** Show Evidence Ledger and explain source confidence/limitations. Mention that newly discovered stories do **not** automatically become verified cases. Ask members for a real workflow where student teams could conduct a scoped evaluation or case study.

## Five standout cases for this audience
- Walmart — frontline adoption and practical workflow redesign (company-reported results)
- Microsoft/Accenture developer trials — experimentally measured task completion
- Providence St Joseph Health — modest documentation benefits, evidence limitations
- McDonald's — a stopped pilot is meaningful evidence, not just a failure story
- iTutorGroup — accountability and fairness in automated decisions

## What board members should remember
1. Cases are **illustrations**, not a measured AI success-rate sample.
2. A reported savings claim is not the same as an independently demonstrated net economic benefit.
3. AI literacy is not just prompt writing: skills include evaluation, workflows, data quality, privacy, risk and judgment.
4. The dashboard is a living research tool, but **approval remains human-gated**.
